Legendary Characters of Prehistory

A collection of legendary and myth-inspired figures imagined in a
prehistoric world. These characters are fictional composites, not claims
about real prehistoric people.

1. Aruk, the First Firekeeper

Aruk was said to be the first person of his clan to protect an ember
through a long winter. While others feared the dark, he learned to carry
fire in a clay-lined shell and teach others how to feed it. His story
represents ingenuity, cooperation, and the discovery of ways to survive
harsh environments.

2. Nara, the Cave Painter

Nara was a gifted artist who painted animals, hunters, and strange
symbols on the walls of a limestone cave. According to the legend, she
believed that remembering an animal’s shape helped the clan understand
its habits. Her paintings became a shared record of migrations, seasons,
and important hunts.

3. Thorg, the Mammoth Hunter

Thorg was a patient hunter who studied mammoth trails rather than
relying on strength alone. He taught his companions to work together,
guide large animals away from dangerous ground, and use every part of an
animal after a successful hunt. In later stories, he became a symbol of
courage guided by wisdom.

4. Eira, the Star Watcher

Eira watched the night sky and noticed that certain stars returned with
the changing seasons. She used these patterns to help her people
anticipate cold weather and plan journeys. Her legend celebrates
curiosity and the earliest forms of practical sky-watching.

5. Kovan, the Stone Shaper

Kovan was renowned for making sharp, balanced stone tools. He
experimented with different stones and taught apprentices how to strike
flakes safely and precisely. The clan remembered him not as a warrior,
but as a craftsperson whose work made daily life easier.

6. Sura, the Keeper of Seeds

Sura gathered wild grains and edible plants, carefully remembering where
each grew and when they ripened. During a difficult season, her
knowledge helped the clan find food when game was scarce. Some versions
of the tale say she began scattering useful seeds near the camp, hinting
at the first steps toward cultivation.

7. Dagan, the Great Tracker

Dagan could read faint marks in mud, broken branches, and disturbed
grass. He used these clues to locate animals and find safe routes across
unfamiliar land. His stories teach that close observation can be more
valuable than speed or force.

8. Mira, the Healer of the Valley

Mira knew which leaves soothed irritated skin and which roots could be
prepared as simple remedies. She cared for injured hunters and helped
elders during difficult journeys. Her legend honors the importance of
shared knowledge and caring for vulnerable members of a community.

9. Orun, the Bridge Builder

Orun found a way to cross a deep stream using fallen trunks, vines, and
carefully placed stones. The crossing connected two seasonal camps and
made travel safer. In the legends, Orun represents cooperation,
planning, and the human desire to overcome natural obstacles.

10. The White Stag

The White Stag was a mysterious animal said to appear before major
changes in the seasons. Some stories describe it as a guide that led
lost travelers toward shelter; others warn that anyone who chased it out
of greed would lose their way. It symbolizes the mystery and spiritual
meaning people may attach to the natural world.

A Note on History

Prehistoric societies left archaeological evidence such as tools, cave
art, footprints, burials, and settlements. The characters in this file
are creative inventions inspired by broad themes of prehistoric life.
They should not be treated as documented historical individuals.
